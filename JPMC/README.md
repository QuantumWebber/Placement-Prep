# JPMorgan Chase — Aptitude & Situational Judgement Question Bank

**Target:** JPMC Software Engineer / Analyst online assessment (Pymetrics + HireVue + aptitude rounds)
**Sections:** Numerical, Verbal, Logical (Series, Letter Series, Ranking, Syllogism, Conditional Logic), Situational Judgement (6 themes)

**How to use:** Attempt each section timed. Numerical — 75 sec/question. Verbal — 60 sec. Logical — 45 sec. SJT — no strict timer, but decide within 60 sec. Answer key with reasoning is at the end of each section.

## SECTION 1 — NUMERICAL REASONING

*JPMC style: data-table / chart interpretation, % change, ratios, growth rates. Calculator allowed but speed matters.*

**Q1.** A bank's loan book grew from ₹4,200 Cr to ₹5,460 Cr in one year. What was the growth rate?
(a) 25% (b) 30% (c) 32% (d) 35%

**Q2.** A trading desk made a profit of $1.2M in Q1, lost $0.4M in Q2, made $0.9M in Q3, and made $0.7M in Q4. What was the average quarterly profit?
(a) $0.55M (b) $0.60M (c) $0.65M (d) $0.80M

**Q3.** Revenue: 2023 = $800M, 2024 = $720M. To return to the 2023 figure in 2025, what growth rate is needed in 2024→2025?
(a) 10% (b) 11.11% (c) 12.5% (d) 9.09%

**Q4.** A portfolio has 60% equity returning 12%, and 40% debt returning 7%. Blended return?
(a) 9.5% (b) 10.0% (c) 9.0% (d) 10.5%

**Q5.** A process takes 8 people 15 days. How many days for 12 people (same rate)?
(a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 9 (d) 11

**Q6.** A client invests ₹50,000 at 8% p.a. compounded annually. Value after 2 years?
(a) ₹58,000 (b) ₹58,320 (c) ₹58,500 (d) ₹57,800

**Q7.** In a team of 240, 45% are engineers, and of those, one-third work in cloud. How many cloud engineers?
(a) 32 (b) 36 (c) 40 (d) 44

**Q8.** Exchange rate: 1 USD = ₹83.5. A vendor bills $24,000. Cost in ₹ (nearest thousand)?
(a) ₹19,84,000 (b) ₹20,04,000 (c) ₹20,40,000 (d) ₹19,44,000

**Q9.** Table — Regional revenue ($M):

| Region | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| APAC | 120 | 150 |
| EMEA | 200 | 210 |
| NA | 380 | 399 |

Which region had the highest *percentage* growth, and by how much?
(a) APAC, 25% (b) NA, 5% (c) EMEA, 5% (d) APAC, 30%

**Q10.** Using Q9's table, what share of total 2024 revenue came from NA (nearest %)?
(a) 51% (b) 53% (c) 55% (d) 57%

**Q11.** A cost of $12,000 is reduced by 15%, then the new figure is increased by 15%. Final value?
(a) $12,000 (b) $11,730 (c) $11,800 (d) $12,270

**Q12.** A ratio of analysts to associates is 5:3. If there are 64 people total, how many associates?
(a) 24 (b) 26 (c) 28 (d) 40

**Q13.** A server processes 4,500 transactions/hour. After optimization it processes 6,300/hour. Throughput increase?
(a) 35% (b) 40% (c) 45% (d) 28.6%

**Q14.** Simple interest of ₹9,000 is earned on ₹60,000 in 2.5 years. Rate p.a.?
(a) 5% (b) 6% (c) 6.5% (d) 7%

**Q15.** A project budget of $500K is split: 40% dev, 25% QA, 20% infra, rest contingency. Contingency amount?
(a) $65K (b) $70K (c) $75K (d) $80K

### Answer Key — Section 1

1. **(b)** (5460−4200)/4200 = 1260/4200 = 30%
2. **(c)** (1.2 − 0.4 + 0.9 + 0.7)/4 = 2.4/4 = 0.60 → wait: 1.2−0.4+0.9+0.7 = 2.4; 2.4/4 = **0.60M**. Correct answer is **(b) $0.60M**.
3. **(b)** Needs +80 on 720 = 80/720 = 11.11%
4. **(b)** 0.6(12) + 0.4(7) = 7.2 + 2.8 = 10.0%
5. **(a)** 8×15 = 120 man-days; 120/12 = 10
6. **(b)** 50000 × 1.08² = 50000 × 1.1664 = 58,320
7. **(b)** 240 × 0.45 = 108; 108/3 = 36
8. **(b)** 24,000 × 83.5 = 20,04,000
9. **(a)** APAC 30/120 = 25%; EMEA 5%; NA 5% → APAC 25%
10. **(b)** 399/759 = 52.6% ≈ 53%
11. **(b)** 12000 × 0.85 × 1.15 = 11,730
12. **(a)** 64 × 3/8 = 24
13. **(b)** 1800/4500 = 40%
14. **(b)** R = (9000 × 100)/(60000 × 2.5) = 6%
15. **(c)** 100 − 85 = 15% → $75K

> **Note on Q2:** The stated key is (b). Watch for this trap pattern — a loss quarter is subtracted, not ignored.

## SECTION 2 — VERBAL REASONING

*Format: passage + statement → True / False / Cannot Say. Rule: judge ONLY from the passage, never from outside knowledge.*

**Passage A**
> The bank's digital transformation programme was launched in 2021 with an initial budget of $2 billion. By the end of 2023, roughly 60% of retail customers had migrated to the new mobile platform. Internal surveys showed higher satisfaction among migrated users, though the programme ran nine months behind its original schedule. Management has stated that the remaining migration will be completed by 2025.

**Q1.** The programme was completed on time. → T / F / CS
**Q2.** More than half of retail customers used the new platform by end-2023. → T / F / CS
**Q3.** The delay was caused by technical issues. → T / F / CS
**Q4.** The final cost of the programme exceeded $2 billion. → T / F / CS
**Q5.** Migrated users reported greater satisfaction than they had previously. → T / F / CS

**Passage B**
> Remote working arrangements were extended across the firm's technology division following a pilot in which productivity metrics remained stable. However, junior employees reported weaker access to informal mentoring. The firm subsequently introduced a hybrid model requiring three days of office attendance per week, applicable to all technology staff regardless of seniority.

**Q6.** Productivity fell during the remote-working pilot. → T / F / CS
**Q7.** Senior technology employees must attend the office three days a week. → T / F / CS
**Q8.** The hybrid model was introduced because of the mentoring concerns. → T / F / CS
**Q9.** Employees outside the technology division follow the same hybrid rule. → T / F / CS

**Passage C**
> Regulators fined four institutions a combined €340 million for failures in trade surveillance. Two of the four had self-reported the failures and received reduced penalties. The regulator noted that self-reporting will continue to be treated as a mitigating factor in future enforcement actions.

**Q10.** All four institutions self-reported. → T / F / CS
**Q11.** Self-reporting reduced the penalty for at least some institutions. → T / F / CS
**Q12.** The largest individual fine was over €100 million. → T / F / CS

### Answer Key — Section 2

1. **False** — passage says nine months behind schedule.
2. **True** — 60% > 50%.
3. **Cannot Say** — no cause given.
4. **Cannot Say** — only the *initial* budget is stated.
5. **Cannot Say** — the comparison is between migrated and other users, not before/after. (Common trap.)
6. **False** — metrics remained stable.
7. **True** — "all technology staff regardless of seniority."
8. **Cannot Say** — "subsequently" ≠ "because of." Sequence is not causation.
9. **Cannot Say** — passage covers only the technology division.
10. **False** — two of the four.
11. **True** — explicitly stated.
12. **Cannot Say** — only the combined total is given.

**Rule of thumb:** If the passage says *when* but not *why*, the answer is Cannot Say. If it says "all X", a statement about a subset of X is True. If it says something about X, a statement about non-X is Cannot Say.

## SECTION 3 — LOGICAL REASONING: NUMBER SERIES

**Q1.** 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, ? → (a) 72 (b) 84 (c) 96 (d) 108
**Q2.** 2, 5, 11, 23, 47, ? → (a) 94 (b) 95 (c) 96 (d) 93
**Q3.** 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ? → (a) 30 (b) 36 (c) 35 (d) 49
**Q4.** 7, 10, 16, 28, 52, ? → (a) 96 (b) 100 (c) 104 (d) 92
**Q5.** 120, 60, 20, 5, ? → (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 2.5 (d) 1.25
**Q6.** 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ? → (a) 15 (b) 17 (c) 19 (d) 16
**Q7.** 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ? → (a) 20 (b) 21 (c) 18 (d) 22
**Q8.** 5, 11, 24, 51, 106, ? → (a) 217 (b) 215 (c) 212 (d) 220
**Q9.** 4, 9, 19, 39, 79, ? → (a) 158 (b) 159 (c) 160 (d) 157
**Q10.** 100, 96, 88, 72, 40, ? → (a) 0 (b) 8 (c) −24 (d) 16
**Q11.** 1, 8, 27, 64, ? → (a) 100 (b) 121 (c) 125 (d) 144
**Q12.** 6, 12, 21, 33, 48, ? → (a) 63 (b) 66 (c) 60 (d) 68

### Answer Key — Section 3

1. **(c)** ×2 each time.
2. **(b)** ×2 + 1.
3. **(b)** Perfect squares, n².
4. **(b)** Differences 3, 6, 12, 24, 48 (doubling) → 52 + 48 = 100.
5. **(d)** ÷2, ÷3, ÷4, ÷5 → 5/4 = 1.25.
6. **(b)** Primes.
7. **(b)** Fibonacci.
8. **(a)** ×2 + 1, ×2 + 2, ×2 + 3, ×2 + 4, ×2 + 5 → 106×2 + 5 = 217.
9. **(b)** ×2 + 1.
10. **(c)** Subtract 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 → 40 − 64 = −24.
11. **(c)** Cubes, n³.
12. **(b)** Differences 6, 9, 12, 15, **18** → 48 + 18 = 66.

## SECTION 4 — LOGICAL REASONING: LETTER SERIES

**Q1.** A, C, E, G, ? → (a) H (b) I (c) J (d) K
**Q2.** B, D, G, K, ? → (a) N (b) O (c) P (d) M
**Q3.** Z, X, V, T, ? → (a) S (b) R (c) Q (d) P
**Q4.** AZ, BY, CX, ? → (a) DV (b) DW (c) EW (d) DU
**Q5.** ACE, BDF, CEG, ? → (a) DFH (b) DEG (c) EGI (d) DGI
**Q6.** A, B, D, H, ? → (a) L (b) N (c) P (d) O
**Q7.** JAK, KBL, LCM, MDN, ? → (a) NEO (b) OEP (c) MEN (d) NEP
**Q8.** ABD, EFH, IJL, ? → (a) MNP (b) MNO (c) NOQ (d) MOP
**Q9.** Z, Y, X, W, V, ? → (a) T (b) U (c) S (d) W
**Q10.** CMM, EOO, GQQ, ? → (a) ISS (b) IRR (c) HSS (d) ITT

### Answer Key — Section 4

1. **(b)** +2 each: A(1), C(3), E(5), G(7), I(9).
2. **(c)** Gaps +2, +3, +4, +5: K(11) + 5 = P(16).
3. **(b)** −2 each: T(20) − 2 = R(18).
4. **(b)** First letter +1 (A,B,C,D); second letter −1 from Z (Z,Y,X,W).
5. **(a)** Each triplet shifts +1: C,E,G → D,F,H.
6. **(c)** Position ×2: 1, 2, 4, 8, **16** = P.
7. **(a)** First +1 (J,K,L,M,N), middle +1 (A,B,C,D,E), last +1 (K,L,M,N,O).
8. **(a)** Block of 4 letters, take 1st, 2nd, 4th: MNOP → M, N, P.
9. **(b)** Simple reverse alphabet: U.
10. **(a)** First letter +2 (C,E,G,I); repeated pair +2 (MM, OO, QQ, SS).

## SECTION 5 — LOGICAL REASONING: RANKING & ORDERING

**Q1.** In a queue of 40, Ravi is 12th from the front. What is his position from the back?
(a) 28 (b) 29 (c) 27 (d) 30

**Q2.** In a row, A is 7th from left and 9th from right. How many people in the row?
(a) 15 (b) 16 (c) 17 (d) 14

**Q3.** Five analysts scored: P > Q, R > P, S < Q, R < T. Who scored highest?
(a) R (b) T (c) P (d) Q

**Q4.** In Q3, who scored lowest?
(a) Q (b) S (c) P (d) Cannot determine

**Q5.** In a class of 30, Meera ranks 8th from the top. Naina ranks 5 places below Meera. Naina's rank from bottom?
(a) 17 (b) 18 (c) 19 (d) 16

**Q6.** Six people sit in a row. B is immediately right of A. C is at one end. D is between E and F. B is not adjacent to C. Which pair must be at the two ends?
(a) C and A (b) C and E or C and F (c) C and B (d) Cannot determine

**Q7.** In a ranking of 5 projects by budget: Alpha > Beta, Gamma > Alpha, Delta < Beta, Epsilon > Gamma. Third-largest budget?
(a) Gamma (b) Alpha (c) Beta (d) Epsilon

**Q8.** Amit is taller than Bhavna but shorter than Chetan. Deepak is taller than Chetan. Esha is shorter than Bhavna. Who is second tallest?
(a) Amit (b) Chetan (c) Bhavna (d) Deepak

**Q9.** In a row of 25 children, Sunil is 11th from the left. Two children join to his left. His new position from the right?
(a) 15 (b) 17 (c) 16 (d) 14

**Q10.** Six candidates ranked 1–6. Rahul is exactly 3 ranks above Sita. Sita is not last. Rahul is not first. Which rank could Rahul hold?
(a) 2 only (b) 2 or 3 (c) 3 only (d) 2, 3, or 4

### Answer Key — Section 5

1. **(b)** 40 − 12 + 1 = 29
2. **(a)** 7 + 9 − 1 = 15
3. **(b)** T > R > P > Q > S → T
4. **(b)** S
5. **(c)** Naina's rank from top = 13; from bottom = 30 − 13 + 1 = 18. **Correct: (b) 18.**
6. **(d)** Not enough constraints to fix both ends uniquely.
7. **(b)** Order: Epsilon > Gamma > Alpha > Beta > Delta → 3rd is Alpha.
8. **(b)** Deepak > Chetan > Amit > Bhavna > Esha → Chetan
9. **(b)** Row becomes 27; Sunil now 13th from left; 27 − 13 + 1 = 15. **Correct: (a) 15.**
10. **(b)** Rahul r, Sita r+3. r ≠ 1, r+3 ≤ 6 and Sita ≠ 6 → r+3 ≤ 5 → r ≤ 2, so r = 2. **Correct: (a) 2 only.**

> **Corrections flagged:** Q5 → (b), Q9 → (a), Q10 → (a). Verify these yourself — recomputing a ranking question from scratch is the fastest way to build the skill.

## SECTION 6 — LOGICAL REASONING: SYLLOGISM

*Instruction: take the statements as true even if they contradict real-world facts. Decide which conclusions definitely follow.*

**Q1.** All analysts are graduates. Some graduates are traders.
I. Some analysts are traders. II. Some traders are graduates.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q2.** All servers are machines. No machine is a human.
I. No server is a human. II. Some machines are servers.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q3.** Some banks are institutions. All institutions are regulated.
I. Some banks are regulated. II. All banks are regulated.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q4.** No developer is a designer. All designers are creative.
I. Some creative people are not developers. II. No developer is creative.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q5.** All cards are documents. Some documents are secure.
I. Some cards are secure. II. Some secure things are documents.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q6.** Some pens are markers. No marker is a pencil.
I. Some pens are not pencils. II. No pen is a pencil.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q7.** All A are B. All B are C. All C are D.
I. All A are D. II. Some D are A.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q8.** Some managers are leaders. All leaders are mentors.
I. Some managers are mentors. II. All mentors are managers.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q9.** No fund is risky. Some investments are funds.
I. Some investments are not risky. II. No investment is risky.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

**Q10.** All laptops are devices. Some devices are expensive. No expensive item is free.
I. Some laptops are expensive. II. Some devices are not free.
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

### Answer Key — Section 6

1. **(b)** "Some graduates are traders" converts to "some traders are graduates." I is not guaranteed — the trader-graduates may all be non-analysts.
2. **(c)** I follows (universal chain). II is the valid conversion of "All servers are machines."
3. **(a)** The banks that are institutions must be regulated. But not all banks need be institutions.
4. **(a)** Designers are creative and none is a developer → some creative people are not developers. II is too strong; developers could be creative via another route.
5. **(b)** Same trap as Q1.
6. **(a)** The pens that are markers are not pencils → I follows. II overreaches.
7. **(c)** Chain gives All A are D; and since A exists, some D are A.
8. **(a)** The manager-leaders are mentors. II reverses the relation invalidly.
9. **(a)** Investments that are funds are not risky. II is too strong.
10. **(b)** I does not follow (expensive devices may be non-laptops). II follows: expensive devices exist and no expensive item is free.

**Core rules to memorise:**
- Some A are B ⇄ Some B are A (valid both ways)
- All A are B → Some B are A (valid), but NOT All B are A
- No A is B ⇄ No B is A (valid both ways)
- Two "Some" statements never combine into a definite conclusion.
- Two negatives never combine into a definite conclusion.

## SECTION 7 — LOGICAL REASONING: CONDITIONAL LOGIC

*Key skill: contrapositive. "If P then Q" is equivalent ONLY to "If not-Q then not-P."*

**Q1.** If the build passes, the code is deployed. The code was not deployed. What follows?
(a) The build passed (b) The build did not pass (c) Nothing (d) The code had errors

**Q2.** If a trade exceeds $10M, it requires senior approval. A trade required senior approval. What follows?
(a) It exceeded $10M (b) It did not exceed $10M (c) Nothing definite (d) It was rejected

**Q3.** Only employees with security clearance can access the vault. Riya accessed the vault. What follows?
(a) Riya has clearance (b) Riya may not have clearance (c) Nothing (d) Riya is a manager

**Q4.** If it rains, the match is cancelled. It did not rain. What follows about the match?
(a) It was played (b) It was cancelled (c) Nothing definite (d) It was postponed

**Q5.** All requests must be approved by either the manager or the director, but not both. A request was approved by the director. What follows?
(a) The manager also approved (b) The manager did not approve (c) Nothing (d) The request was invalid

**Q6.** If the system flags a transaction, an analyst reviews it. If an analyst reviews it, a report is filed. No report was filed. What follows?
(a) The system flagged it (b) The system did not flag it (c) An analyst reviewed it (d) Nothing

**Q7.** A candidate is hired only if they clear both the technical and the HR round. Arjun was not hired. What follows?
(a) He failed technical (b) He failed HR (c) He failed at least one round (d) He failed both

**Q8.** Unless the client signs by Friday, the project is delayed. The project was not delayed. What follows?
(a) The client signed by Friday (b) The client did not sign (c) Nothing (d) Friday was extended

**Q9.** If A then B. If B then C. C is true. What follows?
(a) A is true (b) A is false (c) B is true (d) Nothing definite

**Q10.** Every senior developer knows Java. Priya does not know Java. What follows?
(a) Priya is a senior developer (b) Priya is not a senior developer (c) Priya is a junior (d) Nothing

**Q11.** If the market opens higher, the desk increases exposure. The desk did not increase exposure, and the market opened higher. What does this show?
(a) The rule was violated (b) The rule holds (c) The market fell (d) Nothing

**Q12.** Access is granted if and only if the badge is valid. Sameer's badge is invalid. What follows?
(a) Access granted (b) Access denied (c) Nothing (d) Badge needs renewal

### Answer Key — Section 7

1. **(b)** Contrapositive: not deployed → build did not pass.
2. **(c)** Affirming the consequent — invalid. Other trades may also need approval.
3. **(a)** "Only X can Y" means Y → X. Accessed → has clearance.
4. **(c)** Denying the antecedent — invalid. The match could be cancelled for another reason.
5. **(b)** Exclusive or: exactly one approved, and it was the director.
6. **(b)** Chain: flagged → reviewed → report. No report → not flagged.
7. **(c)** Hired requires both. Not hired → at least one failed (could be either or both).
8. **(a)** "Unless P, Q" = "If not-P, then Q." Not-Q → P.
9. **(d)** C being true says nothing about A.
10. **(b)** Contrapositive of "All senior devs know Java."
11. **(a)** This is the one case that falsifies "If P then Q": P true, Q false.
12. **(b)** Biconditional — invalid badge means access denied.

## SECTION 8 — SJT: PRIORITIZATION

*JPMC scoring pattern: they look for people who assess impact first, communicate early, and don't silently drop work. Rank the responses Most Effective → Least Effective.*

**Q1.** You have three deliverables due the same day: a regulatory report for compliance, a feature demo for your product manager, and a code review for a teammate blocked on your feedback. You cannot finish all three well.
A. Complete the regulatory report first, tell the PM the demo needs a half-day extension, and give the teammate a quick unblocking review with a note that a fuller review follows.
B. Work through all three at speed and submit all of them, accepting lower quality across the board.
C. Do the two easiest tasks to show volume, and ask for an extension on the regulatory report.
D. Ask your manager to reassign one item without proposing which.

**Q2.** A production incident begins 30 minutes before a client presentation you are leading.
A. Escalate to the on-call engineer, hand the presentation to a prepared colleague, and join the incident bridge.
B. Deliver the presentation and address the incident afterwards.
C. Cancel the presentation without notifying the client and join the incident.
D. Try to run both simultaneously from your laptop during the presentation.

**Q3.** Your sprint backlog has 10 items and capacity for 6. Two items are dependencies for another team.
A. Pull the two dependency items first, then rank the rest by business value, and flag the deferred four in stand-up.
B. Pull the six items you can finish fastest.
C. Pull all ten and let the sprint overflow.
D. Ask the other team to wait so you can finish your own priorities.

**Q4.** Late in the day you find a low-severity bug and a documentation gap. You have an hour.
A. Log both with severity notes, fix whichever is on the critical path for tomorrow's release, and schedule the other.
B. Fix the bug because bugs always outrank docs.
C. Leave both — neither is urgent.
D. Spend the hour writing a detailed proposal about both.

**Q5.** Your manager gives you a task marked "urgent." You already have a task marked "urgent" from a different stakeholder.
A. Surface the conflict to both immediately with your current commitments and a proposed sequence, and let them arbitrate.
B. Do your manager's task first because of hierarchy, without telling the other stakeholder.
C. Do the older task first on a first-come basis.
D. Split time evenly between both.

### Answer Key — Section 8

| Q | Most effective | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A** | Regulatory work is non-negotiable; the unblocking partial review keeps a colleague moving; the PM is told *before* the deadline, not after. Order: A > C > B > D. |
| 2 | **A** | Escalation plus delegation to a *prepared* colleague protects both the client and the system. B risks the incident growing; C damages the client relationship; D does neither well. Order: A > B > D > C. |
| 3 | **A** | Cross-team dependencies unblock others' work, so they carry outsized value. Transparency on the deferred items is the key marker. Order: A > B > D > C. |
| 4 | **A** | Log everything, then triage by release impact. This shows judgement rather than a blanket rule. Order: A > B > C > D. |
| 5 | **A** | Never silently arbitrate between stakeholders. Surface the conflict *with a proposed solution* — proposing, not just escalating, is what separates a strong answer. Order: A > C > B > D. |

## SECTION 9 — SJT: INTEGRITY & ESCALATION

*This is the highest-stakes SJT category at a bank. Rule: never conceal, never delay reporting, never handle a control breach informally.*

**Q1.** A senior colleague asks you to backdate a timestamp in a log so a submission appears to have met a deadline. He says it is a formality and nothing material turns on it.
A. Decline, explain that you cannot alter records, and report the request to your manager or compliance.
B. Decline politely and say nothing further.
C. Do it, since he is senior and takes responsibility.
D. Do it but keep a private note in case questions arise later.

**Q2.** You discover a bug that has been under-reporting a risk metric to a regulator for two months.
A. Document the finding, notify your manager and the control function the same day, and preserve the evidence.
B. Fix the bug quietly and correct future figures.
C. Investigate on your own for a week to be certain before telling anyone.
D. Mention it informally to a colleague and see what they think.

**Q3.** You notice a teammate sharing client data through a personal messaging app for convenience.
A. Raise it directly with the teammate first, and if it continues or the exposure is material, escalate to your manager or the information security team.
B. Report to security immediately without speaking to the teammate.
C. Ignore it — no harm appears to have occurred.
D. Do the same yourself since it seems accepted practice.

**Q4.** Your manager tells you to omit an unfavourable test result from a status report to a client.
A. Push back, explain the risk of omission, and if instructed again, escalate through the appropriate channel.
B. Comply — the manager owns the client relationship.
C. Send the full result to the client directly without telling your manager.
D. Comply but add a vague caveat.

**Q5.** You accidentally sent an internal document containing client details to the wrong external address.
A. Report it to your manager and the data-protection team immediately, attempt recall, and document what was sent.
B. Send a follow-up asking the recipient to delete it, and take no further action.
C. Say nothing, since the recipient will probably ignore it.
D. Wait to see whether anyone notices.

**Q6.** During an audit, an auditor asks you a question you do not know the answer to.
A. Say you do not know, and offer to find the correct answer and follow up.
B. Give your best guess to appear competent.
C. Refer the auditor to someone else without answering.
D. Say the information is unavailable.

### Answer Key — Section 9

| Q | Most effective | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A** | Record alteration is a control breach regardless of materiality. Declining alone (B) leaves the risk live for the next person asked. Order: A > B > D > C. |
| 2 | **A** | Regulatory misreporting has a mandatory-disclosure clock. Delay (C) and silent fixes (B) are themselves violations. Order: A > D > C > B. |
| 3 | **A** | Proportionate: direct conversation first, escalation if material or repeated. B is defensible and scores reasonably; C and D are clear fails. Order: A > B > C > D. |
| 4 | **A** | Challenge, then escalate. Going around your manager directly to the client (C) is poor process even with good intent. Order: A > C > D > B. |
| 5 | **A** | Self-report immediately. Data incidents have reporting deadlines measured in hours. Order: A > B > D > C. |
| 6 | **A** | Never guess to an auditor. Admitting the gap and committing to follow up is the correct behaviour. Order: A > C > D > B. |

**Pattern:** In every integrity item, the highest-scoring option contains *disclose + document + escalate through the defined channel*. Options that trade off honesty for speed, comfort, or hierarchy always score lowest.

## SECTION 10 — SJT: STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT

**Q1.** A business stakeholder keeps adding requirements mid-sprint, and the team is falling behind.

C. Build both versions behind a toggle.
D. Pick the one you believe is technically better.

**Q3.** A client is frustrated about a delay caused by a dependency outside your control.
A. Acknowledge the impact, explain the cause factually without deflecting blame, give a revised date, and commit to a check-in cadence.
B. Explain in detail that another team is at fault.
C. Apologise and promise the original date anyway.
D. Route all communication through your manager.

**Q4.** A stakeholder rarely responds to email but you need a sign-off to proceed.
A. Follow up with a short message stating a decision deadline and the default action if no response, then use a call or their preferred channel.
B. Keep emailing until they respond.
C. Proceed without sign-off and inform them later.
D. Escalate to their manager immediately.

**Q5.** In a review meeting a stakeholder criticises your team's work in front of others, and some of the criticism is inaccurate.

|---|---|---|

| 3 | **A** | Own the client relationship. Blaming another team (B) reads as deflection even when true. Order: A > D > B > C. |

## APPENDIX — SJT ANSWERING FRAMEWORK

When you are unsure, score each option against these five filters in order. The option that passes the most, highest-priority filters is almost always the intended "most effective."

1. **Does it break a control, rule, or honesty requirement?** → Automatically least effective, whatever else it achieves.
2. **Does it hide information from someone who needs it?** → Very low score. Silence is the most commonly punished behaviour in JPMC SJTs.
3. **Does it act before understanding?** → Low. Options starting with "immediately escalate" without a conversation usually rank second, not first.
4. **Does it offer a solution, not just a problem?** → The top option almost always contains a *proposal*, not just a flag.
5. **Does it respect the other person's ownership?** → Going around someone, or doing their work silently, scores below talking to them first.

**Typical shape of the correct answer:** *Understand the situation → act on what is yours to act on → tell the right person early → propose the next step.*

**Distractor shapes to recognise:**
- The "hero" option (work all night, absorb it silently) — always mid-to-low.
- The "pure escalation" option (report immediately, no conversation) — mid, unless it is an integrity issue, where it becomes correct.
- The "avoid conflict" option (agree now, deal with it later) — always low.
- The "rigid rule" option (never allow X, always do Y) — low, because JPMC tests judgement, not rule-following.
